# Copy Quality, Style & Writing Rules

> Distilled from direct-response copywriting training material.

---

## The #1 Rule: Lower Reading Level = Higher Conversions

- Average American reads at 7th grade level
- But they're not giving full attention to ads -- so write at 3rd-4th grade level
- Gary Halbert (greatest copywriter ever): off-the-charts vocabulary, consistently wrote at 3rd grade level
- Stefan Georgi (greatest living copywriter): lowering reading level had PROFOUND impact on conversions

**Keep it as low as possible. Always.**

---

## Clear & Concise: Kill the Fluff

**"Fluff" = any extra language that blurs the meaning.**

It takes punchy, crisp copy and makes it long, drawn out, and slow.

### How to eliminate fluff:

1. **Use active voice, not passive:**
   - Active: "The cat chased the mouse." (strong verb: "chased")
   - Passive: "The mouse was chased by the cat." (weak verb: "was")

2. **Avoid repetition like the plague.** Every line must be NECESSARY -- expanding benefits, drawing up new pain points, or moving the story forward.

3. **Cut unnecessary details.** If a detail doesn't move toward the sale, delete it.
   - Chekhov's rule: "If in the first act you have hung a pistol on the wall, then in the following one it should be fired. Otherwise don't put it there."

4. **After writing, cut 5-10% of the total copy.** This always makes it stronger.

### Weak vs Strong:

**Weak:** "Because it has the power to really help you..."

**Strong:** "This will completely transform your smile from something you need to hide every time you laugh to bright, white teeth that will make you feel ready for any close-up your kids want to post on Facebook."

The strong version is LONGER but it's not fluff -- every word earns its place through specificity and dimensionalization.

## Conversational Tone

Copy must feel like a conversation with a friend, not a corporate brochure.

### How to achieve conversational flow:

1. **Mix short and long sentences.** Vary rhythm.
2. **Use conversational inflections** (sprinkle, don't overdo):
   - "Okay, but check this out..."
   - "You know what?"
   - "Guess what..."
   - "Listen..."
   - "Here's the thing..."
   - "And the best part?"
   - "Think about it..."
   - "What's even crazier is..."
   - "No kidding..."
   - "The craziest part is..."

3. **Use transitions:** Anyway, However, Also, Plus, Moving On...

4. **Remind them of the promise** throughout -- relate back to the solution they're about to discover.

5. **Check in with the reader** -- reflect their thoughts:
   - "Now of course, if you're here listening to all this... You might be saying, 'Dude, just go get surgery!'"
   - "Something that I bet you're aware of too."
   - "Now look, if you're hearing all this and it sounds a little sketchy, then I get it."

6. **Use "conversational inflections"** to keep copy moving:
   - "See... Look... Now..."
   - "You see what I mean?"
   - "Maybe you can relate..."
   - "Makes sense in theory, right?"

### Example of conversational + visceral copy:

"See, if the problem with our joints is that the fluid between them begins to dry up... slow down... and gradually turn into a rotting swamp.

Then the solution is simply to 'rejuvenate' this fluid so it can return to its normally clear, flowing state...

If you do that, you can soothe and cool any soreness...

You can restore joint mobility, like running a stiff, dry sponge under water...

And you can create a smooth, slippery surface for your joints to glide across each other effortlessly.

Makes sense in theory, right?"

## The Quality Bar for Language

The best descriptions are: **punchy, visual, and visceral.**

- Not "transformed my entire quality of life" (weak, generic)
- But "transform your smile from something you need to hide every time you laugh to bright, white teeth" (specific, visual, dimensional)

- Not "it has the power to really help you" (empty)
- But "you'll walk down the stairs to your kitchen with ease" (concrete, visual)

### The test: Can the reader SEE it in their mind? If not, rewrite.

---

## Copy Style Summary (The Rules)

1. Write at 3rd-4th grade reading level
2. Active voice, strong verbs, no existential verbs (was, were, is, are)
3. Every line must earn its place -- if it doesn't sell, delete it
4. Be specific and situational, never generic
5. Conversational tone -- like talking to a friend
6. Mix sentence lengths for rhythm
7. Use proof after every claim (names + numbers)
8. Dimensionalize benefits -- ask "what does that REALLY mean?"
9. Include social situations -- how others perceive the change
10. Use triplets for momentum
11. After finishing, cut 5-10%
12. The language should be punchy, visual, and visceral -- always
